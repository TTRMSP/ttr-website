Does This Sound Familiar?

Your company may already be telling you it needs an IT department.

Most businesses do not wake up one morning and decide they need managed IT. The warning signs show up in everyday work first. If these situations feel familiar, the technology is already costing your team time, focus and productivity.

A new employee starts Monday and nothing is ready.

The laptop still needs to be ordered, Microsoft 365 is not set up, permissions are unclear and someone in the office gets pulled away from their real job to figure it out.

The same technology problems keep coming back.

Printers, WiFi, email, slow computers and recurring application issues get fixed just enough to work again, but nobody owns the larger problem or prevents the next interruption.

Your vendors keep blaming each other.

The internet provider says it is the firewall. The software company says it is the network. The hardware vendor says it is not their problem. Your team gets stuck in the middle.

Hardware gets replaced only after it becomes a problem.

Computers are bought one at a time, equipment ages at different rates and upgrades happen during emergencies instead of being planned and budgeted before productivity suffers.

Remote employees have a different IT experience.

People working from home or another location lose time on VPN, connectivity, access and device issues because nobody has clear ownership of technology outside the main office.

Everyone assumes the backups and security are fine.

Until someone needs a file restored, an account is compromised or an audit question gets asked, leadership may not know exactly what is protected, what is monitored or what recovery would actually look like.

If you recognized your business in this list, that is what TTR is built to take off your plate.

We bring the users, devices, vendors, networks, security and planning into one managed technology relationship.
